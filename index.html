Load, cover and equivalence

Everything is converted through milligrams of active drug: 1 mg of amphetamine base ≈ 2.7 mg of methylphenidate, Adderall salts are 73% amphetamine base, lisdexamfetamine is 29.6% dexamfetamine base, and Concerta 36 mg ≈ 30 mg of immediate-release methylphenidate per day. On that basis Adderall XR 30 mg and lisdexamfetamine 70 mg deliver almost the same amount of base, which is what the published conversion tables pair. They convert total daily load, not curve shape, so any switch is re-titrated on clinical response. The sleep threshold is a modelling convention, not a validated figure: one shared line across the chart, set as a share of the highest curve on screen, with lights out taken as 23:00, so every regimen is read against the same yardstick. The 20% default is the residual that modified-release formulations are engineered to stay under in order to avoid insomnia; no clinical study establishes a level above which a patient cannot sleep, and tolerance to the sleep effect builds over weeks, so a settled patient often sleeps well above it. Modafinil has no established equivalence to stimulants and is drawn on its own scale.
